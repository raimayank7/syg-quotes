Famous quotes that were never said

Some of the most shared quotations in the world were never said by the people they are credited to, or were said in different words. Here are well known examples, with what the record actually shows. None of the doubtful versions is listed as a quotation anywhere else on this site.

Never said, or not in those words

The popular lineCredited toWhat the record shows
"Be the change you wish to see in the world."Mahatma GandhiNot found in his writings in these words. The nearest passage, from 1913, reads: "If we could change ourselves, the tendencies in the world would also change."
"Elementary, my dear Watson."Sherlock HolmesThe phrase does not appear in any of Arthur Conan Doyle's Holmes stories. It became famous through later plays and films.
"Let them eat cake."Marie AntoinetteThere is no evidence she said it. A similar story about "a great princess" appears in Rousseau's Confessions, written when Marie Antoinette was still a child.
"I disapprove of what you say, but I will defend to the death your right to say it."VoltaireWritten by his biographer Evelyn Beatrice Hall in The Friends of Voltaire (1906) to sum up his attitude. Voltaire did not write it.
"Be yourself; everyone else is already taken."Oscar WildeNot found in any of Wilde's works or letters.
"Insanity is doing the same thing over and over and expecting different results."Albert EinsteinNo record that Einstein said or wrote it. The line is first seen in print in the early 1980s, long after his death.
"The ends justify the means."MachiavelliA summary of ideas in The Prince. The sentence itself is not in the book.
"Go confidently in the direction of your dreams. Live the life you have imagined."Henry David ThoreauA shortened rewording of a longer sentence in Walden (1854). The real sentence is on our Thoreau page.

How to check a quotation yourself

  1. Look for a source, not just a name. A real quotation can be traced to a book, a speech, a letter or a dated report. "Gandhi said" with nothing after it is a warning sign.
  2. Search the exact words inside quotation marks together with the name of the work. If the line appears only on quote websites and posters, doubt it.
  3. Check the full text. Shakespeare, Austen, Dickens, Emerson, Thoreau, Tagore and most authors on this site can be read free on Project Gutenberg and Wikisource.
  4. Notice the style. A line that sounds like a modern motivational poster probably is one.
  5. Remember who is speaking. In plays and novels, a line belongs to a character and may be the opposite of what the author believed.

If you find a mistake in any quotation or source on this site, please tell us. We would rather remove a line than keep a wrong one.
