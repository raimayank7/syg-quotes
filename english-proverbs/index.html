English proverbs with meanings

A proverb is a short, old saying that states a piece of common sense. Nobody knows who first said most of them. They have been polished by centuries of use. Each one below is given with its meaning in plain words.

Proverbs are not quotations, so no author is named, except for one or two that began as a writer's line and are marked so. A few well-known ones that can be traced to a writer are on that writer's page instead, such as Franklin's sayings on Poor Richard.

Proverb, idiom or quotation?

  • A proverb is a complete sentence that gives advice or states a general truth: "Actions speak louder than words."
  • An idiom is a phrase whose meaning is different from its words, and it is not a sentence on its own: "a piece of cake", "under the weather".
  • A quotation is the exact words of a known person, with a source: a book, a play, a speech.

In an examination, "expand the proverb" or "write a story on the proverb" means you must explain the general truth and show it at work in an example.

How to expand a proverb in a school answer

  • Meaning: say in one or two sentences of your own what the proverb teaches.
  • Explanation: say why it is true, in three or four sentences.
  • Example: give one short example from daily life, history or a story. The tortoise and the hare, for "Slow and steady wins the race", is the classic one.
  • Conclusion: end with one sentence on how a student can apply it.

Keep it to about a hundred words unless the question asks for more. Do not repeat the proverb in every sentence.

Using a proverb in a speech or essay

One proverb is seasoning. Five are a meal nobody ordered. Use a proverb to open, with a twist of your own, or to close, when the audience has already seen the proof in your examples. Proverbs often come in opposing pairs, such as "Many hands make light work" and "Too many cooks spoil the broth". That is not a fault: each is true in its own situation, and a good speaker knows which situation is in front of them.

Translating proverbs

Most proverbs have a cousin in Hindi and Punjabi that uses a different picture for the same idea. "A bad workman blames his tools" is "नाच न जाने आँगन टेढ़ा". Do not translate word for word. Find the cousin. See our pages of Hindi kahavatein and Punjabi akhaan.

Good to know

  • Learn a proverb with its meaning and one example. That is all an examiner asks for.

Questions

Who wrote these proverbs?

Nobody we can name. Proverbs are folk sayings that have passed from mouth to mouth for centuries. That is the difference between a proverb and a quotation.

Can I use proverbs in an essay?

Yes. One or two, well chosen, show a good command of English. Explain how the proverb fits your point.
